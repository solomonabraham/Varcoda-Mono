# Varcoda Mono — design and engineering specification

Version 1.500 · ten styles (five weights, each with an italic) · 649 glyphs · 579 characters · SIL Open Font License 1.1

This document records what Varcoda Mono is, how it is constructed and why the
main decisions were taken. Every number below is defined in
`scripts/fontsrc/params.py`; the construction rules live in
`scripts/fontsrc/kit.py` and the `glyphs_*.py` modules.

## 1. Design identity

Varcoda Mono is a monospaced typeface for source code. Its brief: the calm,
unambiguous letterforms a programmer needs for long sessions, drawn with a
geometric, technical hand. JetBrains Mono (readability, clear symbols) and
Saira (squared geometry) were named as *references for the direction only* —
no outline, measurement or file of either typeface was used. All glyphs are
generated by the code in this repository.

The identity rests on five rules that are applied everywhere:

1. **Superelliptic bowls.** Every curve is a quarter arc between a horizontal
   and a vertical tangent, with a handle tension of 0.62 (a circle is 0.5523).
   Bowls therefore have straight sides, a short flat top and bottom, and tight
   but smooth corners — a "squircle". On-curve points sit on extremes only.
2. **Flat shoulders.** Where an arch or bowl leaves a stem (n m h u r, b d p q,
   a g), its outer edge stays level with the end of the stem instead of
   dipping into a notch; only the far corner is round. The inner corner at the
   stem is eased by a small fillet of constant visible width (34–40 units in
   every weight).
3. **Orthogonal terminals.** Strokes end with a horizontal or a vertical cut,
   never an oblique one (c e s a r, 2 3 5 6 9, C G S J). Diagonals are cut
   horizontally at the baseline and at their top line.
4. **Squircle dots** for i j and all punctuation.
5. **An elliptical pen.** Vertical stems are slightly heavier than horizontal
   strokes (ratio 0.89 in Light to 0.81 in Bold); diagonals fall in between
   automatically. Glyphs with three stacked horizontals (a e s, B E S 3 8 &)
   get a further reduction in the heavier weights so their counters stay open.

## 2. Grid and metrics

Units per em: 1000. Advance width of **every** glyph: 600.

| Line | y | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Line box top | 980 | hhea ascender = OS/2 typo ascender |
| Ascender | 772 | b d f h k l (756 before 1.200) |
| Cap height / figures | 720 | |
| x-height | 540 | 75 % of the cap height |
| Operator axis | 320 | centre of + − = < > ~ * and arrows |
| Baseline | 0 | |
| Descender | −192 | g j p q y |
| Line box bottom | −250 | hhea descender = OS/2 typo descender |
| Bracket span | −160 … 800 | ( ) [ ] { } / \ \|, centred on the axis |
| Overshoot | 8 | round shapes only; flat shoulders use none |

Line gap is 0 and `USE_TYPO_METRICS` is set, so the default line height is
1.23 em on every platform. `usWinAscent` / `usWinDescent` are 1010 / 280: the
Windows clipping box additionally contains the overshoot of the box-drawing
glyphs (see section 7).

### The five pens

| | Light | Regular | Medium | SemiBold | Bold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| usWeightClass | 300 | 400 | 500 | 600 | 700 |
| Lowercase stem (vertical) | 60 | 84 | 97 | 110 | 124 |
| Lowercase horizontal | 52 | 70 | 80 | 90 | 100 |
| Capital / figure stem | 64 | 90 | 104 | 118 | 133 |
| Capital / figure horizontal | 56 | 75 | 85 | 95 | 106 |
| Left edge of n (side bearing) | 94 | 84 | 79 | 74 | 68 |
| Left edge of H | 77 | 67 | 62 | 56 | 50 |
| Left edge of figures | 90 | 83 | 78 | 74 | 70 |
| Dot of i j / punctuation dot | 90 / 129 | 115 / 150 | 128 / 161 | 142 / 172 | 156 / 184 |

A master is defined by its stem alone; all other values are interpolated
between two anchor pens (stems 54 and 124). Capitals and figures are about 7 % heavier
than the lowercase so that text has an even colour. Letters grow outwards as
they get bolder (side bearings shrink) to keep the counters open.

Version 1.200 re-drew the weight scale after measuring the family against other coding fonts
(see `QUALITY_REPORT.md`): Regular went from a 78-unit to an 84-unit stem, Light from 54 to 60,
and SemiBold was added.

## 3. Construction

Glyphs are small Python functions built from a handful of parts:

- `ring`, `bowl`, `arch` — superelliptic strokes expanded from a centre line
  with the elliptical pen; the offset curves are fitted as single cubics that
  keep the node structure of the skeleton.
- `rect`, `diag`, `slash`, `pline` — straight strokes; diagonals take their
  thickness from the same pen and are cut horizontally.
- Boolean union / intersection / difference (skia-pathops) to join parts and
  to cut terminals.

After the last boolean operation every outline is cleaned: overlaps removed,
outer contours counter-clockwise, coordinates rounded to integers, collinear
points merged, contours smaller than 60 square units and segments shorter than
2.6 units removed. The test-suite checks that no such debris reaches the
sources and that every glyph has the same number of contours in all weights.

## 4. Character differentiation

| Group | How they differ |
| --- | --- |
| 0 O o Ø | zero is narrower and carries a dot: an upright pill as wide as a stem in the middle of the counter. A slash separates 0 from O just as well but makes it a look-alike of Ø, so the slashed zero (side to side of the counter, as heavy as a stem in Light) is the `zero` feature. |
| 1 l I \| | 1: flag and foot bar · l: head serif and a curved tail to the right · I: full slabs top and bottom · bar: plain, taller, descending |
| 5 S | 5 has a flat top bar and a sharp upper-left corner |
| 8 B | 8 has a clear waist (its upper ring is visibly narrower than the lower one); B has a straight stem with square corners |
| D O | D has square left corners, tighter right corners than O and no overshoot |
| g q 9 | single-storey g with a flat open tail; q is a plain stem; 9 is a rotated 6 with a closed bowl |
| . , : ; | comma and semicolon have a long, sturdy tail reaching about −170 units |
| \` ' " | the backtick is wide and clearly slanted; quotes are straight ticks |
| - _ | hyphen sits on the operator axis and is as wide as the bars of + and =; the underscore leaves a 108-unit gap so that `__` stays countable |

`tests/` measures these pairs both on the outlines and on hinted bitmaps at
12–16 px.

## 5. Programming symbols

- + − = < > ~ * ÷ × ≠ ≤ ≥ and the arrows share one vertical centre (y = 320)
  and one width (the operator bars span 95 … 505 in Regular), so `->`, `=>`,
  `<=`, `+=`, `-=` line up without ligatures.
- ( ) [ ] { } / \ | share one vertical span, centred on the same axis.
- `#` is upright; `$` has strokes above and below a slightly smaller S instead of a through-stroke (longer since 1.500);
  `%`, `@` and `&` use reduced pens so that their counters stay open in Bold.
- The asterisk is six-pointed and centred on the axis, which suits `*=`,
  `/* */` and pointer syntax better than a superscript star.

## 6. OpenType features

| Tag | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `calt` | programming ligatures (optional, see below) |
| `zero` | slashed zero (the default zero is dotted; until 1.300 the dotted zero was `ss01`, and that number is not reused) |
| `ss02` | single-storey a (also for à á â ã ä å ā ă ą) |
| `ss03` | plain zero without slash or dot (added in 1.200) |
| `ss04` | l with a flat foot instead of the curved tail (added in 1.200; plain `l` only) |
| `ss05` | squared y, built like u with a hooked descender (added in 1.200; also ý ÿ ŷ) |
| `ss06` | plain q, the mirror image of p (the default q has a tail; added in 1.200) |
| `ss07` | m with all three legs on the baseline (the default m has a short middle leg; added in 1.200) |

There is no kerning and no positioning feature: nothing can move a glyph off
the grid.

### Ligatures

`!=` `!==` `==` `===` `=>` `->` `<-` `<->` `<=` `>=` `<=>` `&&` `||` `??`
`++` `--` `::` `//` `/*` `*/` `|>` `<|`, since 1.200 `:=` `**` `?.` `+=` `-=`, since 1.400 `*=`, since 1.500 `|=`

- **Optional.** They live in `calt`, the feature that the ligature switches
  of editors and terminals control. With `calt` off every operator is the
  plain glyph and the font is complete without them.
- **Cell-preserving.** A ligature for *n* characters still occupies *n*
  glyphs: the first *n* − 1 become the empty spacer `LIG`, the last becomes a
  ligature glyph that is 600 units wide and draws backwards over the
  preceding cells. Cursor positions, column counts and advance widths never
  change (tested with 400 random operator strings per style).
- **Conservative.** Longer runs are left alone: `====`, `---`, `///`, `:::`,
  `>>=`, `<<=`, `-->`, `==>` and similar sequences stay plain.
- **Compact comparisons.** `<=` and `>=` are 720 units wide and centred on the
  border between their two cells, with white space on both sides. Until 1.300
  they filled both cells (980 units); the chevron then opened by only 17° and
  stopped reading as `<`. It now opens by 33° (the plain `<` by 54°).

Decision: `calt` rather than `dlig`. `dlig` would be off everywhere by
default, but the "Enable ligatures" switch of JetBrains IDEs and
`editor.fontLigatures: true` in VS Code only reach `liga`/`calt`. VS Code and
JetBrains IDEs keep ligatures off until asked; in applications that apply
`calt` by default (for example Windows Terminal) they can be switched off
with a font-feature setting — see the README.

Accented letters are composites of a base letter and one of 26 accent
components (lowercase and capital forms of each accent). Cedilla, ogonek and
all barred letters are merged outlines. ď ľ ť Ľ use the apostrophe-shaped
caron; ģ takes a turned comma above.

Box-drawing glyphs fill the whole cell (0 … 600 × −250 … 980) and reach 20
units further horizontally and 30 units further vertically on every side that
touches the cell boundary. Renderers round cell sizes up to whole pixels;
without this overlap that rounding leaves hairline gaps between rows and
columns. The glyphs are left unhinted so that they are not shifted.

## 8. Formats, naming, hinting

| | TrueType (`fonts/ttf/*.ttf`) | OpenType/CFF (`fonts/otf/*.otf`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outlines | quadratic, converted from the cubic sources (max. error 0.8 unit) | cubic, as drawn |
| Hinting | ttfautohint (hinting range 6–48 ppem, x-height rounded up for sizes up to 14 ppem) | AFDKO otfautohint stem hints; alignment zones −8/0, 540/548, 720/728, 756/764, −200/−192 |
| Accents | composite glyphs | decomposed (26 component-only glyphs omitted) |

The TTFs are the recommended files on Windows.

Names: family "Varcoda Mono"; Regular and Bold are style-linked; Light and
Medium carry the legacy family names "Varcoda Mono Light" / "Varcoda Mono
Medium" plus typographic family/subfamily (name IDs 16/17), so that both old
(GDI) and new (DirectWrite) font menus group them correctly. PostScript names
are `VarcodaMono-Light`, `-Regular`, `-Medium`, `-Bold`.

Monospace flags: `post.isFixedPitch` = 1, PANOSE proportion = 9,
`OS/2.xAvgCharWidth` = 600, `hhea.advanceWidthMax` = 600,
`hhea.numberOfHMetrics` = 3 (OpenType recommendation for monospaced fonts),
CFF `isFixedPitch`. Glyph names follow the AGLFN, otherwise `uniXXXX`.

## 9. Sources and build

```
glyph programs (scripts/fontsrc/glyphs_*.py)
   └─ draw_master()  ─►  sources/VarcodaMono-<Style>.ufo   (UFO 3, cubic, features.fea)
                              └─ ufo2ft ─► TTF ─► ttfautohint ─► fonts/ttf/VarcodaMono-<Style>.ttf
                              └─ ufo2ft ─► OTF ─► otfautohint ─► fonts/otf/VarcodaMono-<Style>.otf
```

The UFOs are complete, ordinary sources (outlines, components, metrics, names
and feature code) and open in Glyphs, FontForge, RoboFont and FontLab.
`build-fonts.py --from-ufo` compiles hand-edited UFOs without regenerating
them. Builds are reproducible: timestamps are fixed
(`SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` is honoured) and the test-suite rebuilds two styles and
compares the files byte for byte.

## 10. Decisions taken where the brief left room

| Question | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source format | Parametric Python that writes UFO | one construction for all weights, repeatable builds, still editable in font editors |
| Advance width | 600 / 1000 | the common coding-font proportion; integer cell at 10, 15, 20 px |
| x-height | 540 (75 % of cap) | large enough for 12–14 px, still clearly smaller than capitals |
| Arch joins | flush, eased inner corner | crisp on the pixel grid, no notch to clog, gives the family its look |
| Zero | dotted by default, slashed as `zero`, plain as `ss03` | a slashed zero is read as Ø by Danish and Norwegian readers (reader feedback) |
| g | single-storey, flat open tail | simplest robust form at small sizes, distinct from q and 9 |
| a | double-storey by default, single-storey as `ss02` | keeps a apart from o at small sizes |
| `#` | upright | renders sharper than a slanted one at 12–16 px |
| Soft hyphen U+00AD | visible hyphen | an invisible character in source code is a hazard |
| Ligatures | `calt`, cell-preserving | works with the ligature switches of the target editors |
| Line metrics | 980 / −250, gap 0, typo metrics on | same line height on all platforms |
| Vendor ID | `NONE` | no registered vendor tag is claimed |
| Italic | a written lowercase; capitals, figures and signs sloped | comments and keywords must differ from code at a glance and still sit on the same grid |
| Kerning | none | would break monospaced alignment |

## 11. Known limitations

See the "Known limitations" section of the README, which is kept current: an italic sloped by a shear, no
variable font (97 of 649 glyphs are not yet interpolation-compatible), Nerd Font icons only as a separate download, automatic hinting
only, precomposed accents only, Latin only, and no editor or terminal driven on Windows or macOS so far.

## 12. The Saira direction

Saira is the primary visual reference: rounded-rectangle bowls, square dots, tall ascenders, an industrial,
technical tone. What was carried over, with numbers, and where the family deliberately departs from Saira,
is in `docs/BRANDING.md`. Nothing was copied; Saira was measured (`scripts/compare-fonts.py`) and looked at.

Details that are Varcoda's own: the blade cut on extender ends, flat shoulders, softened square dots, the
tailed `q` (not the mirror image of `p`), the short middle leg of `m`, the hooked `l`, a `D` squarer than `O`.

## 13. Italic (since 1.300, written since 1.400)

The italic is produced by the same program as the upright. The lowercase is drawn in its own, written
construction (`scripts/fontsrc/glyphs_italic.py`); everything alphabetic is then slanted (`italic.py`).

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Slant | 9.5° clockwise, pivoting on the middle of the x-height so lowercase stays centred in the cell |
| Exit stroke | a stem that reaches the baseline leaves it to the right: `a d h i k m n u`. It reaches 90 units beyond the stem (51 in `m`) and stands flat on the baseline. The roman `l` and `t` already end this way |
| Branch | an arch grows out of its stem at 46 % of the x-height and has 74 % of the full weight there: `h m n r`, the bowls of `b p`. The roman meets the stem with a flat shoulder instead |
| Return | the same curve turned by 180°: a bowl runs back up into its stem: `a d g q u y` |
| Pen-cut tops | stems that start at the x-height (`m n p r u y`) begin with the blade cut of the ascenders |
| `f` | descends to the descender line and ends in a hook to the left that answers the one at the top |
| Room | letters with an exit stroke are 27 units narrower on the right, so the stroke fills the gap to the next letter instead of crowding it |
| Kept from the roman | `c e j l o s t v w x z` (no stem standing on the baseline), all capitals, figures and signs, sloped |
| Stays upright | box drawing, block elements, arrows, geometric shapes, check marks, the radical, Powerline |
| Follows the base letter | accented letters, `ı ŋ þ ª µ` and the alternates are built from the italic forms; `æ œ ß ĳ ĸ ſ` keep their roman construction |
| Metrics | identical line box, x-height and advance to the upright; `post.italicAngle` −9.5, caret slope set |
| Linking | Regular / Italic / Bold / Bold Italic form one group; Light, Medium and SemiBold each pair with their italic; a STAT table describes both axes |

Why this form. Until 1.300 the italic was the roman, slanted, with three substituted letters; it read as an
oblique. The brief for 1.400 asked for an italic with a hand in it that still reads at 12–16 px. The three
gestures above are the smallest set that changes the texture of a line (they touch fifteen of the twenty-six
letters, among them most of the frequent ones) while keeping what makes the family: orthogonal terminals,
superelliptic corners, the blade cut. Each gesture already exists in the roman, which is why the two styles
sit together in one line of code.

Considered and not drawn: a looped `k`, a script `s`, round `v` and `w`. At 12–14 px the loop of `k` fills in,
and a round `v` comes too close to `u`; both would trade legibility in code for expression.

Punctuation, brackets and operators are sloped, not redrawn. They keep their size, their axis and their cell,
so a comment that quotes code shows the same signs as the code.

Limits: the slant is a shear, so curve weight is not optically corrected (under 3 % of the stem); wide
capitals lean up to 52 units over the right cell edge, the top of `f` up to 24.

## 14. Texture healing: evaluated, not implemented

Texture healing lets a narrow letter give room to a wide neighbour by swapping in alternate glyphs through
`calt`, while every advance stays the same. It was evaluated for this family and left out:

1. **The measured need is small.** The variation of stem-to-stem gaps in lowercase text is 35 %, level with
   or better than JetBrains Mono (37 %), Fira Code (36 %) and Iosevka (39 %). The crowding of `m` is already
   reduced by its short middle leg.
2. **It needs glyphs that reach into the neighbouring cell.** Applications that draw or clip every cell
   separately can cut such glyphs. For a font whose first promise is "nothing leaves the cell", that is the
   wrong trade.
3. **Cost and fragility.** It needs two extra drawings of every affected letter in ten styles and a
   context state machine in `calt` that must not interfere with the 29 ligatures already there.

If it is revisited, it should follow the variable-font work, be a separate opt-in feature, and be judged
on rendered text in terminals as well as editors.
